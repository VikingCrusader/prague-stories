# Illustration guide (History Timeline)

Moved out of CLAUDE.md on 2026-10-18 to keep it under the size limit. Read this before writing any History Timeline illustration prompt.

## Illustration production rules

- **Base prompt template**: wide 16:9 (1024×576), a flat aged-tan parchment-textured frame band with Celtic-knot corner flourishes that fills the canvas edge to edge (not a "scroll" or sheet of paper; see "No white outer margin" below), painterly high-detail pixel art (not blocky 8-bit), one wide panoramic scene (no inset "vision-within-a-vision"), and a bottom caption plaque described as *"a simple, perfectly flat rectangular parchment-colored bar... straight parallel edges... not tilted or in perspective"* holding a **short** bold serif all-caps caption, rendered once with no repeated words. Offer 2–4 caption options instead of guessing.
- **Standard frame (user's call, 2026-10-07, applies to every new illustration):** the reference is `crowned-while-his-father-watched-1376.webp` ("SEVEN VOTES, NO CONTEST"): an aged parchment band of about 30px on a 1024px image (a thin dark outer edge, then tan parchment, then a dark inner line), slightly worn and scuffed, with Celtic-knot corner pieces and the plaque overlapping up into the artwork. "About half the usual width" was tried first and came out far too thin (`the-compacts-chased-down-1448`'s first image, about 8px on black), so describe it as *"a moderately narrow aged parchment frame band, slimmer than a heavy ornamental border but clearly visible and wide enough for the Celtic-knot corner pieces to sit comfortably on it; the parchment looks old, slightly worn and scuffed, with faint stains and a few small nicks along its outer edge"*, and keep the outer edge filling the canvas (any outside area pure black, never white). Images made before this date keep their old, inconsistent borders (measured 8 to 60+px, some torn-edged); the user decided to leave them until the second pass after the whole timeline is drafted. A batch re-frame in code (the 1376 frame as a template) was prototyped and parked: old corner knots overlap the artwork and would double up, and frame/plaque detection failed on 3 of 9 samples.
- **Frame constants (old wide frame, superseded by the narrow frame above)** (measured off `egerland-endgame-1322.webp`): border **48px on all four sides**; caption plaque **height 50px**, sitting on the bottom border band; plaque width = text + ~100px padding per side, max ~928px, min ~350px. **Leak gotcha:** literal numbers ("48px", "~928px", "~350px") have been rendered as visible text inside the artwork. Keep the numeric frame spec in its own block separate from the scene description, or describe sizing qualitatively ("sized generously to fit the caption with comfortable padding, neither cramped nor absurdly wide"), and always end with: *"do not render any sizing instructions, measurements, numbers, or annotations as visible text anywhere in the image — this describes the frame, it is not text to display."* Also state "no readable text/scale markers/annotations anywhere in the artwork."
- **No white gap**: state *"the illustrated scene must extend directly to the inner edge of the parchment border, with zero gap — no white, cream, or blank margin line of any kind between the border's inner edge and the artwork."*
- **No white outer margin** (2026-09-27): "parchment scroll border" makes the model draw a torn-edged sheet floating on a white canvas, leaving white around the ragged outer edge and in the corners. The page background is dark, so white there stands out badly. Describe the border as a flat frame band that fills the canvas to the outermost pixel, with straight, clean outer edges (no torn, ragged or curled edges, no drop shadow). Add a black fallback: *"if any area outside the frame is unavoidable, it must be solid pure black, never white or light grey."* Also avoid the word "scroll". The prompt alone doesn't reliably fix it, so run every generated image through `python client/scripts/fillMargins.py <in> <out.webp>` (native `E:\` paths): it floods from the image border through near-white pixels only and fills them with `--bg-card` (`#111428`, the `.history-detail-panel` background), so the margin disappears into the page. It also writes the WebP, so it replaces the Pillow conversion step below.
- **Caption text**: a dropped negation reads as a plausible sentence ("NOT EVEN CAESAR WROTE THIS" came back without "NOT") — double-check any caption built on a negation. `imageCaptions[i]` (the small line under the image) should describe the scene plainly, not echo the plaque text.
- **Character consistency**: write the full locked description word-for-word in every prompt — "the same character as before" fails (Wenceslas came back with a different face). When a locked-design figure is in a scene but *not* the foreground figure, say explicitly and by name that no other figure shares his distinguishing features (Žižka's eyepatch/fur hat/fur cloak bled onto the nearest figure once).
- **Architecture/setting anchors**: no Gothic spires before 1344; Prague skyline = red-tiled rooftops up the hill, Vltava in golden light, stone arch bridge, scaffolded St. Vitus silhouette on the castle hill. The user knowingly accepted a bridge in pre-1357 skylines (Charles Bridge didn't exist yet) — don't re-litigate; from 1357 on, bridge and cathedral can both be shown under construction with no hedging. When depicting the very first stone of the bridge, state that no completed multi-arch bridge may appear anywhere.
- **Battle prompts**: say explicitly that a routed army is fleeing *away* (it rendered as a second charging wave); Luxembourg arms are barry silver-and-blue with a red lion (it rendered a modern tricolor); a "blind" figure must have eyes closed/covered.
- **Hussite banners**: always a **yellow cup-shaped chalice on a red field** (Tábor's own, Žižka's included — the old red-field/white-chalice variant is retired). Describe the chalice as an actual cup-shaped icon; naming only the colors gives a plain red flag.
- **17th-century gunpowder war (from 1618)**: to keep Thirty Years' War scenes clearly apart from the Hussite ones, list what belongs (bronze siege cannon on wheeled carriages behind wicker gabions, zigzag siege trenches, matchlock musketeers firing from forked rests with bandoliers, pike squares in morions and breastplates, buff coats and broad felt hats, pistol cavalry, heavy powder smoke) and what must not appear (wagon forts, chalice banners, flails, crossbows, knights in full plate, hand-cannons on poles). Worked first pass on `a-first-victory-1618`. Banners: imperial = black double-headed eagle on gold; Bohemian = white crowned lion with a forked double tail on red; Savoy = white cross on red, arms running to all four edges of a wide banner, ideally with a ducal coronet (a short floating cross reads as Switzerland, an off-centre one as Denmark; name it in the caption). Describe each emblem as an icon, not just colours.
- **Arms of the five Crown lands (accepted first pass on `the-diet-decides-1619`, "THE DIET DECIDES")**: reuse verbatim. Bohemia: RED field, SILVER-WHITE LION rampant with a FORKED DOUBLE TAIL and a GOLD CROWN. Moravia: BLUE field, crowned EAGLE CHEQUERED RED AND WHITE. Silesia: GOLD field, crowned BLACK EAGLE with ONE head and a narrow SILVER CRESCENT across chest and wings. Upper Lusatia: BLUE field, GOLD CRENELLATED WALL. Lower Lusatia: WHITE field, RED OX walking. Add "none of the banners shows a double-headed eagle" so neither eagle drifts into the Habsburg one.
- **Early hand-cannons (1419–20)**: a short plain metal tube on a straight unshaped wooden pole, no stock/curved grip/trigger, must not resemble a musket; two men — one braces the pole rear against his chest/under an arm/against a wagon edge, the second touches a smoldering match-cord to a touch-hole near the rear. Fold this into one complete prompt rather than iterating in small amendments. Evolve the look as the wars progress.
- **Real-photo "artifact" frame**: a second, deterministic (Python/Pillow, not AI) treatment for genuine photographs/objects — dark museum-wall background with soft vignette, photo letterboxed inside a thin gold double-line frame with small gold diamond corners, brass-gradient caption plaque in Georgia Bold. Used for the 1997 100-koruna banknote on `father-of-the-fatherland-1378`. The original script lived in an old scratchpad; rebuild the logic in a fresh scratchpad file. Keep the Celtic-knot parchment style for all generated scenes.
- **Format**: if a generated image arrives as `.jpg`, convert with Python + Pillow (`Image.open(...).convert('RGB').save(path, 'WEBP', quality=90)`) — no `cwebp`/ImageMagick/ffmpeg on this machine; use native `E:\...` paths, not Git Bash paths. After adding an image, set `image` in `seedHistoryEvents.js` *and* live via `HistoryEvent.updateOne({ slug }, { $set: { image } })` (`$setOnInsert` won't touch seeded docs).
